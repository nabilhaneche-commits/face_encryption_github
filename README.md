A novel 4D ReLU-memristive hyperchaotic system: Hidden attractors, multistability, and transient chaos with application to face recognition encryption
Haneche Nabil, Hamaizia Tayeb
Abstract
A secure color image encryption framework for face recognition is presented, built upon a novel 4D
ReLU-memristive hyperchaotic system and an advanced DNA diffusion mechanism. Integrating
the ReLU-type memristor into the Liu chaotic system yields a higher-order autonomous system
exhibiting hidden attractors across a wide parameter range. Attractor complexity is analyzed via
phase portraits, Poincar´e sections, Lyapunov spectra, and bifurcation diagrams. Multistability
and transient chaos are detailed to highlight the system’s dynamical richness. The complexity of
the generated hyperchaotic sequences is further quantified via spectral entropy and C0 complexity
metrics, confirming high structural unpredictability. The hyperchaotic sequences are validated by
the NIST SP 800-22 randomness suite. Encryption first detects the face region via a Haar cascade,
then applies a 12-bit DNA diffusion based on complementary base pairing exclusively to that
region, while the full image undergoes a dual-layer global diffusion using coupled hyperchaotic
sequences, followed by a hyperchaotic Fisher–Yates permutation and inter-channel obfuscation.
Experimental results demonstrate that the face-targeted design reduces encryption time by over
53% compared to full-frame encryption, while achieving information entropy above 7.99, nearzero
pixel correlation, NPCR exceeding 99.60%, and UACI around 33.41%, confirming strong
resistance to statistical and differential attacks. The scheme also exhibits robustness against noise
and cropping attacks, validating its suitability for real-time secure transmission of facial biometric
data.
